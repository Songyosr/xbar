# Teaching Statistics with Interactive Applets

Interactive applets can transform how students learn statistical concepts. Here are strategies we've seen work well in classrooms.

## Before the Applet

**Set the Context**
- Introduce the problem or question
- Connect to real-world examples
- Preview what students will discover

**Prime for Exploration**
- "What do you think will happen if we increase sample size?"
- "How might changing this parameter affect the result?"

## During Exploration

**Guided Discovery**
1. Start with default settings
2. Change one parameter at a time
3. Make predictions before clicking
4. Discuss observations as a group

**Common Patterns**
- Let students explore freely first
- Then provide specific tasks
- Always connect back to theory

## After the Activity

**Consolidation**
- What surprised you?
- How does this connect to the formula?
- When would you use this in practice?

## Technical Tips

- Test applets beforehand on your classroom setup
- Have backup static visualizations ready
- Consider pairing students for discussion

The goal isn't to replace theory—it's to make abstract concepts concrete and memorable.
